# Full rule set

Source: r/EngineeringResumes wiki (index, typo, templates, sample-bullet-points pages), plus the
cscareerquestions SWE resume guide and Nick Singh's 36 rules. US/Canada conventions.

## Read-and-skim rules

1. Single-column layout. No icons, images, graphics, charts, skill bars, headshots.
2. Don't indent sections or bullets — bullets already indent themselves.
3. Bullets, never paragraphs.
4. Modern readable font (Calibri, Charter, Carlito, Arial, Lato, Helvetica), black, ≥10.5pt, not thin.
5. Don't justify text — it creates uneven word spacing.
6. Margins ≥ 0.4in, line spacing ≥ 1.07. Enough white space; no cramming.
7. Italics, bold, and ALL CAPS used sparingly and never stacked on each other.
8. Clear visual separation between sections and between jobs.
9. One page. Two only at senior/staff+ (rule of thumb: one page per decade of experience).
10. Skills separated by commas.
11. Export as PDF. Filename: `First Last Company Resume.pdf` when sending.

## Section order

- Working full-time: `Experience > Skills > Education` or `Skills > Experience > Education`
- Student / new grad: `Education > Experience > Skills` (add `Projects` before Skills if experience is thin)
- No technical work experience: `Education > Projects > Experience > Skills`
- No experience at all: `Education > Projects > Volunteer/Extracurriculars > Skills`
- No summary/objective unless senior/staff+, career change, or employment gap.
- No references section, no "References available upon request".

## Contact information

- One email only, modern provider (Gmail/Outlook — not AOL/Hotmail/Yahoo). Drop the .edu address after graduation
  unless the school is elite.
- Plain text links: `github.com/username`, `myportfolio.com`, `name@gmail.com`. No `https://www.`,
  no `Email:` / `Phone:` / `Portfolio:` prefixes, no underline/italic/color.
- No street address or ZIP. No city/state unless the job is in that city (non-local location invites bias).
- LinkedIn and phone number are considered unnecessary; if you keep the phone, drop the country code in US/Canada.
- Omit an empty GitHub or a stale portfolio.
- Security clearance goes near the name ("Top Secret / SCI eligible with CI Polygraph").
- Work authorization near the name if it isn't obvious ("US Citizen", "US Permanent Resident").

## Bias

Leave out age, gender, children, nationality, ethnicity, marital status, religion, political views, photo.

## Work Experience

- Section is named `Experience` or `Work Experience`.
- Paid work only (research counts). Label internships as internships, contracts as contracts.
- Order by relevance to the target role, then impressiveness. Best material first.
- Tailor to the job description each time.
- Add context — your resume is not your job description. Accomplishments, metrics, technical wins.
- Don't center a bullet on a tool ("used SolidWorks"); center it on the engineering behind the tool.
- 3–5 bullets per role, more only for a long tenure.
- New grads: show mastery of fundamental engineering work before leadership/PM material.
- Sensitive/classified work: describe the technology and the standard, not the program name.

## Education

- Degree, school, location, graduation date. Reverse chronological (master's above bachelor's).
- No start dates or date ranges — graduation only. `Expected May 2027` if still enrolled.
- "Bachelor of Science", "Master of Science" — spelled out.
- No high school. No schools you didn't finish.
- No coursework unless the course is genuinely exotic. Standard curriculum is assumed.
- GPA only if ≥ 3.75, two decimals; drop it once you have a full-time job.
- Skip school location when the name contains it (MIT, UCLA) or it's obvious.
- No awards/scholarships unless Rhodes/Fulbright tier. D1/competitive sports can stay.

## Skills

- Section named `Skills`.
- Only languages/technologies you've genuinely used and could be interviewed on.
- Everything listed here should also show up in a bullet point somewhere, and vice versa.
- Order most → least important; question whether the tail belongs at all.
- Optional categories: Languages, Frameworks, Tools, Infrastructure, Design, Simulation, Lab Equipment.
- ≤3 lines, single column, comma separated with proper punctuation — no pipes, slashes, dashes.
- `C, C++` not `C/C++`. Split skills that are functionally different.
- No soft skills (teamwork, leadership, communication) — prove those in bullets.
- No proficiency ratings ("Expert in", 4/5, "Intermediate").
- No assumed skills: Microsoft Word, typing, IDEs (VS Code, vim), operating systems, LaTeX.
- Use `Git`, not `GitHub`/`GitLab`/`Bitbucket`.

### Capitalization (the wiki's typo page)

`C, C++` · `JavaScript` · `TypeScript` · `HTML, CSS` · `C#` (not "C Sharp") · `Node.js` · `.NET` · `Lua` ·
`React` · `Go` · `Git` · `Bash` · `GitHub Actions` · `Jupyter Notebook` · `pandas` · `scikit-learn` ·
`Matplotlib` · `NumPy` · `SciPy` · `PyTorch` · `TensorFlow` · `OpenCV` · `SolidWorks` · `CATIA V5` ·
`AutoCAD` · `MATLAB` · `Autodesk` · `LabVIEW` · `Lean Six Sigma` · `SystemVerilog` · `Power BI` ·
`Fusion 360` · `EAGLE` · `KiCad` · `LTspice` · `FreeRTOS` · `ROS` · `3D Printing` ·
`R` is a language, `RStudio` is an IDE · Git is not a programming language ·
Autodesk and Ansys are companies — name the product (Autodesk Inventor, Ansys Fluent).

## Projects

- Section named `Projects`. Personal/club/hobby projects only — work projects belong under Experience.
- No "Project" in the title, no "(Personal Project)" labels. Capitalize titles properly.
- Roles/dates/locations unnecessary; a link to the repo or a portfolio page matters more.
- Bullet points, ordered by relevance and impressiveness.
- Real projects with users and maintenance beat coursework and tutorial clones. Hiring managers skip
  a Projects section full of tutorials.

## Portfolios / GitHub

- Link only if current. Empty profile or stale portfolio → leave it off.
- Pinned repos need READMEs with a summary, screenshots, how to run, and **how to test**. Include real tests.

## Senior engineers (10+ YoE)

- A ≤2-sentence summary is acceptable.
- Separate resumes for IC and management tracks.
- Cap at 2 pages. Compress early roles. Move Education to the bottom.
- Talk about influence and business results, not just delivery.

## Career changers

- 2-sentence summary explaining the change and motivation.
- Link to working projects and source code. Keep past-career detail short. Fit one page.

## Grammar and proofreading

- Expand obscure abbreviations on first use: "scanning tunneling microscope (STM)".
- Capitalize proper nouns only.
- Digits, not spelled-out numbers.
- Avoid apostrophes, ampersands, and slashes inside bullets.
- Grammar check, not just spell check. Ask a human to proofread.

## ATS reality check

Modern ATSs parse a single-column PDF fine. They do not auto-reject on keyword percentage. Don't stuff
white keyword text, don't build a "keyword" section, don't mangle a readable resume for an imagined robot.
Write for the human; the parseability rules above are all the ATS accommodation needed.
